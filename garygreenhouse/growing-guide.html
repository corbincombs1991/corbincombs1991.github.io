Big baskets.
Happy plants.

Our moss baskets are a favorite for a reason. Give them the support, water, and food they need to flourish.

Ask a growing question ↗
Start with a strong place to hang it.

A fully watered moss basket can weigh 50–75 pounds. Use a hook or bracket rated for the load, attached to a suitable support. As an alternative, remove the hanger and use a large, stable clay pot as a stand.

Give moss baskets a daily check.

Moss baskets dry out quickly as plants grow and days get hotter. Mature baskets may need watering every day. Check moisture regularly and water thoroughly as needed, adjusting for rain, heat, and the basket’s location.

Keep those flowers well fed.

Gary Greenhouse’s moss-basket care guide recommends feeding at least weekly with a water-soluble fertilizer, such as a 20-10-20 formulation. Follow the fertilizer label’s application and dilution directions for your plants.

Make room for early-spring color.

Pansies and violas are cool-weather plants. The greenhouse’s original guide describes properly grown plants tolerating temperatures in the low 20s and upper teens Fahrenheit. Conditions, acclimation, and variety matter; ask us before leaving new plants out in a cold snap.

Find the right spot for your planter.

Our combination planters and hanging baskets include selections for sun and shade. Check the plant tags and ask which mix suits the light where you plan to display it.

Get to know your growing zone.

The USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map helps you choose perennials, shrubs, and trees suited to average winter temperatures. Use your ZIP code in the current official map, and consider your own garden’s light, soil, and exposure.
